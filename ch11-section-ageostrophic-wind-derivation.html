    Section 11.2 Deriving the ageostrophic wind

    Recall from Chapter 8 that the horizontal acceleration of air parcels making up the wind—given by \(\dfrac{Du}{Dt}\) in the zonal direction and \(\dfrac{Dv}{Dt}\) in the meridional direction—has the next-largest magnitude for mid-latitude synoptic scale analysis of the horizontal equations of motion: \(\mathcal{O}(10^{-4} \, \text{m} \, \text{s}^{-2})\text{.}\) Although this horizontal Lagrangian acceleration is about 10 times smaller than the horizontal pressure gradient and Coriolis accelerations for mid-latitude synoptic scale motions, retaining it allows for prediction and explanation of large-scale vertical motions.
    🔗
    Consider the horizontal equations of motion in height coordinates with these three largest accelerations retained:
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Du}{Dt} = -\frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + fv\tag{11.2.1} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Dv}{Dt} = -\frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial y} - fu\tag{11.2.2} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where the Coriolis parameter \(f = 2 \Omega \sin{\phi}\) has been substituted for simplicity. These equations can be combined into vector form as
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D \vec{v}}{Dt} = -\frac{1}{\rho} \nabla_z p - f \, \hat{k} \times \vec{v}\tag{11.2.3} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where the "z" subscript emphasizes height coordinates are used and pressure is measured at constant height.
    🔗
    Unlike the equations of geostrophic balance, which are diagnostic, (11.2.1)–(11.2.3) are prognostic because they feature time derivatives through inclusion of horizontal Lagrangian acceleration. While they are more accurate to real atmospheric motions than the equations of geostrophic balance because the horizontal Lagrangian acceleration is retained, they still are approximate since smaller accelerations such as the curvature terms and viscous friction acceleration are neglected.
    🔗
    Recall from Chapter 8 that the zonal and meridional components of the geostrophic wind are given in height coordinates by
    🔗
    \begin{equation} u_g = -\frac{1}{\rho f} \frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\tag{11.2.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    and
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_g = \frac{1}{\rho f} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\tag{11.2.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    respectively, and its vector equation is given by
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \vec{v}_g = \frac{1}{\rho f} \, \hat{k} \times \nabla_z p\tag{11.2.6} \end{equation}
    🔗
    (11.2.4)–(11.2.6) each can be rearranged to isolate the horizontal pressure gradient acceleration
    🔗
    \begin{equation} f u_g = -\frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\tag{11.2.7} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} -f v_g = -\frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\tag{11.2.8} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} f \, \hat{k} \times \vec{v}_g = -\frac{1}{\rho} \nabla_z p\tag{11.2.9} \end{equation}
    🔗
    for substitution into (11.2.1)–(11.2.3):
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Du}{Dt} = -f v_g + fv = f(v - v_g)\tag{11.2.10} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Dv}{Dt} = f u_g - fu = -f(u - u_g)\tag{11.2.11} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D \vec{v}}{Dt} = f \, \hat{k} \times \vec{v}_g - f \, \hat{k} \times \vec{v} = -f \, \hat{k} \times (\vec{v} - \vec{v}_g)\tag{11.2.12} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Note the vector identity
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \hat{k} \times (\hat{k} \times \vec{A}) = -\vec{A}\tag{11.2.13} \end{equation}
    🔗
    was used to produce (11.2.9).
    🔗
    We define the ageostrophic wind as
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \vec{v}_a = u_a \, \hat{i} + v_a \, \hat{j} = \vec{v} - \vec{v}_g = (u - u_g) \, \hat{i} + (v - v_g) \, \hat{j}\tag{11.2.14} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where \(\vec{v} = u \, \hat{i} + v \, \hat{j}\) is the true horizontal wind. (11.2.14) makes it clear that
    🔗
    \begin{equation} u_a = u - u_g\tag{11.2.15} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_a = v - v_g\tag{11.2.16} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \vec{v}_a = \vec{v} - \vec{v}_g\tag{11.2.17} \end{equation}
    🔗
    In other words, the ageostrophic wind is the deviation of the true horizontal wind from geostrophy, and it results from imbalance between the horizontal pressure gradient force and the horizontal Coriolis force.
    🔗
    Substituting (11.2.15)–(11.2.17) into (11.2.10)–(11.2.12) produces
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Du}{Dt} = f v_a\tag{11.2.18} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{Dv}{Dt} = -f u_a\tag{11.2.19} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D \vec{v}}{Dt} = -f \, \hat{k} \times \vec{v}_a\tag{11.2.20} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Therefore,
    🔗
    \begin{equation} u_a = u - u_g = -\frac{1}{f} \frac{Dv}{Dt}\tag{11.2.21} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} v_a = v - v_g = \frac{1}{f} \frac{Du}{Dt}\tag{11.2.22} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \vec{v}_a = \vec{v} - \vec{v}_g = \frac{1}{f} \, \hat{k} \times \frac{D\vec{v}}{Dt}\tag{11.2.23} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where the vector identity given by (11.2.13) again was used to produce (11.2.23).
