    Section 7.3 Mechanical energy equation

    You learned in your physics course that a change in the mechanical energy (sum of macroscopic kinetic and potential energies) of a closed system is accomplished through work done on the system by non-conservative forces. You may have seen this statement of mechanical energy conservation expressed as
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \Delta K + \Delta U = W_{nc}\tag{7.3.1} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where \(\Delta K = K_2 - K_1\) [J] is the change in kinetic energy \(K\text{,}\) \(\Delta U = U_2 - U_1\) [J] is the change in potential energy \(U\text{,}\) and \(W_{nc}\) [J] is work done on the system by non-conservative forces.
    🔗
    We can build a similar statement of mechanical energy conservation using the vector equation of motion.
    🔗
    Following the derivation of [Martin (2006)], we dot the vector equation of motion with \(\vec{u}\) to produce the following component equations:
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D}{Dt} \left( \frac{u^2}{2} \right) + \frac{u^2 w}{a} - \frac{u^2 v \tan{\phi}}{a} = -\frac{u}{\rho} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + fuv - 2 \Omega uw \cos{\phi} + \nu u \nabla^2 u\tag{7.3.2} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D}{Dt} \left( \frac{v^2}{2} \right) + \frac{v^2 w}{a} + \frac{u^2 v \tan{\phi}}{a} = -\frac{v}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} - fuv + \nu v \nabla^2 v\tag{7.3.3} \end{equation}
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D}{Dt} \left( \frac{w^2}{2} \right) - \frac{w \left(u^2 +v^2 \right)}{a} = -\frac{w}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} - gw + 2 \Omega u w \cos{\phi} + \nu w \nabla^2 w\tag{7.3.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    where the property \(\dfrac{d}{dx} \left( \dfrac{y^2}{2} \right) = y \dfrac{dy}{dx}\) has been applied to the first term on the left-hand side of each equation.
    🔗
    We add (7.3.2)–(7.3.4), which leads to cancellation of all products originating from the curvature and Coriolis terms:
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D}{Dt} \left( \frac{u^2 + v^2 + w^2}{2} \right) = -\frac{1}{\rho} \vec{u} \cdot \nabla p - gw + \nu \vec{u} \cdot \nabla^2 \vec{u}\tag{7.3.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Physically, these terms cancel because they do no work on the wind, and therefore they do not change its kinetic or potential energies.
    🔗
    The term on the left-hand side of (7.3.5) represents the time rate of change of the total kinetic energy per unit mass of an air parcel. Considering this alongside the statement of mechanical energy conservation (7.3.1), the terms on the right-hand side of (7.3.5) must represent the sum of the negative of the time rate of change of the air parcel’s potential energy per unit mass and the time rate of change of the non-conservative work per unit mass being done on the air parcel.
    🔗
    We proceed by rewriting a term on the right-hand side of (7.3.5) to make this connection clearer. Since \(w = \dfrac{Dz}{Dt}\text{,}\) by the definition of geopotential,
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} gw = g \frac{Dz}{Dt} = \frac{D \Phi}{Dt} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    and so (7.3.5) can be written
    🔗
    \begin{equation} \frac{D}{Dt} \left( \frac{u^2 + v^2 + w^2}{2} + \Phi \right) = -\frac{1}{\rho} \vec{u} \cdot \nabla p + \nu \vec{u} \cdot \nabla^2 \vec{u}\tag{7.3.6} \end{equation}
    🔗
    (7.3.6) is the mechanical energy equation. It is not a "new" or "separate" equation: It is a combination and restructuring of the equations of motion. Even so, it reveals what can cause the mechanical energy per unit mass of an air parcel—which is the sum of its kinetic energy per unit mass \(\dfrac{u^2 + v^2 + w^2}{2}\) and its gravity potential energy per unit mass \(\Phi\)—to change.
    🔗
    Note the similarities between the mechanical energy equation (7.3.6) and the equation of mechanical energy conservation (7.3.1). The left-hand side of each equation gives the change in the macroscopic kinetic and potential energies of the air parcel and the right-hand side gives the sum of the work done by non-conservative forces on the air parcel. (Keep in mind that these quantities are intensive (per unit mass) in (7.3.6) but are extensive (dependent on mass) in (7.3.1).)
    🔗
    [Martin (2006)] refers to the first term on the right-hand side of the mechanical energy equation (7.3.6) as pressure work. It represents the mechanical work (per unit mass) done by the pressure gradient force on the air parcel, and it is proportional to pressure advection
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} -\vec{u} \cdot \nabla p \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Consequently, it is positive (negative) for flow directed from higher pressure toward lower pressure (lower pressure toward higher pressure). In other words, it is positive (negative) for positive (negative) pressure advection.
    🔗
    The second term on the right-hand side of the mechanical energy equation (7.3.6) represents the dissipation of energy by viscous friction.
    🔗
    See [Holton and Hakim (2013)] for an alternative derivation of (7.3.6) with viscous friction effects neglected.
